      04 Writing well

      Five rules that get replies

      Professors receive dozens of student emails a week. The ones that get answered are short, specific and personal.

      1. Read before you write. Open one or two of their recent papers and mention something specific.
      2. Ask one clear question. “Please guide me” is hard to answer. “Is your lab taking PhD students for fall 2027?” is easy.
      3. Stay under 150 words. Who you are, why them, what you’re asking.
      4. One professor, one email. Never send the same message to many people at once.
      5. Follow up once. If there’s no reply after two weeks, send one short, polite nudge. Then move on.
      Subject Request for guidance

      Respected Sir/Madam,

      I am a hardworking and passionate student with a keen interest in research. I have attached my CV for your kind consideration. Kindly guide me regarding admission and scholarships and let me know if there is any opportunity in your lab. I am ready to work on any topic.

      Waiting for your kind response.

      Generic · No question they can answer · Could be sent to anyone
      Subject UET Lahore student — question about your paper on low-cost biosensors

      Dear Professor Ahmed,

      I’m in my final year of electrical engineering at UET Lahore, your alma mater. Your recent paper on paper-based biosensors changed how I approached my own final-year project on glucose detection.

      I plan to apply for PhD programs for fall 2027. Is your group expecting to take new students that year, and is there a recent paper you’d suggest I read first?

      Thank you for your time,
      Ayesha Khan

      Specific · Mentions shared alma mater · One easy question · About 70 words

      05 About this directory

      Built carefully, from public sources

      Every entry is sourced

      Each professor holds an appointment at a US university, and their connection to Pakistan comes from a public source such as a university profile, a CV or a published interview. The “connection” line repeats what that source says; it isn’t a label we’ve assigned.

      No email addresses here

      To protect professors from spam and keep their contact details current, we link to each official faculty profile instead. Where the profile publishes an email, we tell you so.

      Professors stay in control

      Are you listed here? You can ask for any change, or to be removed, and we’ll update it promptly.
